Quick explanations

What the vector operations mean in Statics

These are the ideas you should be able to reproduce without the calculator.

How to add vectors

Vectors are added component-by-component. In Statics, this is why several forces can be replaced by one equivalent resultant force.

ExampleA = ⟨30, 40⟩ N and B = ⟨−10, 20⟩ N gives A+B = ⟨20, 60⟩ N.
Common mistakeDo not add force magnitudes unless the forces act along the same line and direction. Add their components instead.

How to find vector magnitude

The magnitude is the length of the vector. For a force, it tells you how large the force is independent of its Cartesian representation.

ExampleFor F = ⟨30, 40⟩ N, |F| = √(30²+40²) = 50 N.
Common mistakeF is often used for the magnitude, while F with vector notation represents the full force vector. Keep the distinction clear in your work.

How force components work

Components are signed projections of a force onto the coordinate axes. If θ is measured from +x, cosine gives the x component and sine gives the y component.

ExampleFor F = 100 N at 30°, Fₓ = 86.60 N and Fᵧ = 50.00 N.
Common mistakeDo not blindly use cosine for x if the angle is measured from a different axis. Draw the triangle first.

What the dot product tells you

The dot product measures how much one vector points in the direction of another. In engineering mechanics it appears in projections and in finding the angle between vectors.

ExampleIf A·B = 0 and both vectors are nonzero, the vectors are perpendicular.
Common mistakeThe dot product returns a scalar, not another vector.
